# Context-fit check

Apply this editorial judgment to every concrete addition, replacement, deletion, or move before presenting an edit proposal or requesting manuscript-edit approval. It covers chapter and scene rewrites, line editing, prose fixes, and AI-associated rewrites in any language. It is a reading and comparison procedure, not a lint rule or a claim that a proposal is automatically correct.

Use the exact candidate, not merely a direction such as "make this natural." Reconcile the complete proposal against the current text, available baselines, and the author's request or accepted finding. Revise an unfit candidate and repeat the check before showing it.

1. **Local continuity:** Read the whole local scene containing the change plus necessary adjacent scenes; extend the reading to relevant earlier setup and later consequences. A fixed line window is neither required nor sufficient. Check references and antecedents, physical, sensory, and temporal claims, spatial relationships, and the surrounding action. Confirm that new wording agrees with adjacent descriptions and the sequence of perception and reaction.
2. **Character and object history:** Check the relevant history, familiarity, knowledge, clothing, possessions, and prior contact in the current manuscript and approved baselines. Distinguish a first observation from a renewed examination, a familiar place from a new one, and new information from something the character already knows or has handled. Keep Story Bible intent distinct from facts established by manuscript text when they conflict.
3. **Repetition:** Check both lexical repetition and repeated action. Compare sentence openings, constructions, gestures, observations, touches, realizations, and emotional beats. Preserve deliberate echoes, refrains, and meaningful reconsideration; change accidental duplication or an action that incorrectly happens for the first time twice.
4. **Voice and collocation:** Read the candidate aloud when useful. Check register, POV, character voice, author's voice, idiom, and word collocation in the actual manuscript language. A locally fluent sentence can still belong to the wrong narrator, genre, situation, or emotional moment.
5. **Intent and narrative dependencies:** Restate the requested outcome or finding's accepted intent in operational terms. Do not turn a request to plant or reinforce a setup into removing or weakening a reveal, or the reverse. Trace affected setups, payoffs, reveals, open threads, and information release in both directions. A deletion must preserve dependent text or name the additional affected paths for author confirmation. Preserve the approved change's meaning and boundaries.
6. **Replacement read:** Put the exact candidate into the surrounding sequence and read the result continuously, including removed text's absence and the transition into the following paragraph. Check every item above against this combined text. Present the resulting candidate with its local context, rationale, relevant uncertainty, and any affected paths rather than an isolated sentence.

If necessary history or dependency evidence is unavailable within the confirmed reading scope, state the uncertainty and request the specific reading or author clarification needed for this proposal; never invent a fact to resolve it. A summary does not replace reading the affected passage. Checking context does not expand the approved edit paths, extend the declared review units, approve additional changes, or authorize a write. Keep unrelated evidence gaps visible under the active strategy's normal gates.
