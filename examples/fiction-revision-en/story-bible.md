# Story Bible: The Tide Archive

> **Fixture notice:** This is an original, handwritten three-chapter fixture for contract validation and real skill exercises. It is not a record of a model session. Structural, character, and sentence defects are intentional; do not treat the current manuscript as a polished target.

## Quick Reference

- **Genre:** Contemporary literary fiction with a coastal setting
- **Target reader:** Adults interested in family conflict, place, and public memory
- **Logline:** On the last night before a ferry terminal holding her town's recorded memories is demolished, an archivist must decide whether preserving her mother's voice matters more than listening to it.
- **Planned length:** Three chapters for this fixture, not a full novel
- **Status:** Deliberately flawed first draft; structure rework requested

## Premise and Theme

Bell Bay's Tide Archive occupies a condemned ferry terminal. It holds analog reels and a failing digital collection of residents' voices. A storm has made the building unsafe, and demolition begins at 06:00. Ning Lin enters at night to retrieve the last recording made by her mother, Mei, and impulsively tries to save the entire collection without help.

**Thematic question:** Does preservation protect a relationship, or can it prevent the relationship from changing?

**Intended moral argument:** A memory remains alive when people consent to share and reinterpret it; keeping an object untouched is not the same as honoring the person connected to it.

**Recurring motifs:** Reel-to-reel tape, the ferry horn, salt on glass, doors and keys, voices overheard through walls.

## Plot Foundation

### Intended Three-Chapter Shape

1. **Chapter 1 — The Last Check-Out:** Establish Ning's private grief and the 06:00 deadline. The failing drive and missing reel force her out of routine cataloging. She refuses Tao's digitizer and locks herself inside, committing to an impossible solo transfer.
2. **Chapter 2 — The Open Reel:** Searching should force Ning and Tao into cooperation. Mei's recorded support for closing the building should act as a Moment of Truth. A power failure should remove the easy digital option, and Ning's choice to broadcast a private recording should create a cost rather than summon a convenient rescue.
3. **Chapter 3 — What the Ferry Carried:** Ning should face the ethical and emotional consequences of the broadcast. Her final decision should cause the external outcome and visibly reject her Lie. The building need not be saved, but the recordings' future must be decided through consent.

### Deliberate Structural Problems to Preserve Until Author Review

- The midpoint supplies new information without a reaction-to-action pivot.
- Mr. Ke arrives with an unplanted key and generator knowledge, removing an obstacle by coincidence.
- The crowd and Han's immediate reversal resolve the external conflict without a decision that costs Ning something.
- Ning's final statement of change is stronger than the dramatized evidence for it.

## Character Registry

### Ning Lin — Protagonist

- **Age / work:** 34, municipal sound archivist
- **Want:** Recover Mei's final tape and keep the archive exactly as it was.
- **Need:** Accept that love does not require freezing a place, a voice, or a family role.
- **Wound:** She ignored Mei's last phone call after an argument and has never listened to the full final recording.
- **Lie:** "If nothing changes, nothing else can be lost."
- **Arc type:** Intended positive arc; currently under-dramatized.
- **Voice:** Precise cataloging vocabulary, short clauses under stress, notices levels and timing before faces, avoids naming grief directly.
- **Relationship with Tao:** She wants his technical help but interprets accepting it as approving his decision to leave Bell Bay.

### Tao Lin — Brother / reluctant ally

- **Age / work:** 31, coastal engineer and consultant to the council
- **Goal:** Move the recordings and prevent his sister from being injured in the terminal.
- **Motivation:** He believes preservation work is meaningless if people are endangered by the container.
- **Method:** Checklists, practical bargains, humor when an argument becomes too personal.
- **Voice:** Concrete verbs, measurements, questions rather than speeches.
- **Consistency flag:** His abrupt cruel joke in Chapter 2 is not adequately caused and clashes with his established method.

### Councilor Han Sorenson — Opposition, not villain

- **Goal:** Close an unsafe building and preserve the collection through a documented move.
- **Motivation:** A previous evacuation injured two residents; he fears repeating it.
- **Method:** Deadlines, access restrictions, public procedure.
- **Why credible:** He authorizes digitization time and knows the collection's value. His instant reversal in Chapter 3 weakens this credibility.

### Mei Lin — Mother, present through recordings

A former ferry announcer who recorded ordinary residents as routes disappeared. She loved the terminal but supported moving the voices into a community-run listening room. Her final tape asks Ning to stop treating the building as her grave.

### Mr. Ke — Former caretaker

Knows the analog equipment. His late arrival with exactly the required key is intentionally convenient; either plant him earlier or replace his rescue with a protagonist-caused solution.

## World Details and Constraints

- **Primary location:** Bell Bay's decommissioned ferry terminal, one night in late autumn.
- **Deadline:** Demolition access ends at 06:00; high water and a damaged sea wall may end it earlier.
- **Technology:** Reel-to-reel decks, an unreliable master drive, a battery loudspeaker system, Tao's portable digitizer.
- **Social rule:** Many recordings are private deposits. Broadcasting them without consent has ethical consequences.
- **Sensory palette:** Ozone from old equipment, salt-stiff curtains, brass keys, ferry diesel, tape hiss, green level lights.
