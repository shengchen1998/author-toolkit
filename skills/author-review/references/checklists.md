# Independent review checklists

Give each reviewer only its assigned checklist and the permitted inputs listed in `SKILL.md`. Every checklist item can produce zero or more findings. A finding needs a reader or craft issue, recommendation, severity, judgment confidence, and the narrowest truthful location scope. Use file or chapter scope for a broad issue, line scope for one line, and passage scope with a short quotation for a passage. Follow the conditional location fields in finding v2 rather than inventing a line or quotation.

## Review-mode applicability

For `final` mode, evaluate every applicable item against the complete book. For `current-scope` mode, evaluate only what the confirmed scope contains. Mark checks that require a complete book `skipped` in the report's `Review coverage` section with the reason `outside current scope`. These include the ending and resolution, complete plot beats and chapter order, full character arcs and payoff chains, whole-book revision-plan coverage, and satisfaction of promises that require later chapters. For `request` mode, evaluate only the declared changed and dependency-affected passages and their necessary local context. Also mark findings that require unread text outside those units `skipped` with the reason `outside request review`; do not infer whole-book or full-target reader experience from them. Evaluate local causality, continuity, prose, comprehension, and reader response only where the supplied text supports them. Mark applicable but incompletely reviewed local coverage `partial`. Never infer missing chapters or describe a current-scope review as a whole-book final review.

## Intent and consistency

Inputs: manuscript, Story Bible, approved draft-baseline when supplied, and approved revision plan.

### Premise and reader contract

- Does the delivered story express the approved premise and intended emotional journey?
- Does the manuscript address the stated target reader and genre expectations established in the Story Bible?
- Are thematic questions and motifs present with the intended meaning rather than replaced by a different implicit argument?

### Plot and structure

- Do major turns, the ending, subplots, and chapter order match the approved baseline?
- Does each planned structural finding have its approved resolution in the manuscript?
- Are causes, stakes, setups, and payoffs consistent with the Story Bible?
- Has an edit introduced an unapproved structural contradiction or gap?

### Characters and world

- Do want, need, wound, lie, arc, voice, and relationships remain consistent with approved character entries?
- Does character knowledge match the timeline and prior scenes?
- Do world rules, locations, objects, dates, and constraints remain internally consistent?
- Are approved archetype and continuity corrections present without flattening intentional character growth?

### Revision plan coverage

- Is every accepted finding ID mapped to a completed, deferred, or dismissed result as the plan records?
- Did an approved change create a new inconsistency elsewhere?
- Are there manuscript changes that contradict the approved plan or Story Bible without a recorded author decision?

## Writing standards

Inputs: manuscript and this checklist. For `language=zh*` or an actually Chinese manuscript, also load [`chinese-writing-standards.md`](chinese-writing-standards.md) and use its Chinese semantic mappings for the English checklist terms below. Follow the actual manuscript when its language differs from the label. Review the text on its own without earlier self-assessment or other reviewers' findings. This Chinese reference belongs only to the standards perspective.

### Structure and scene causality

- Does the midpoint change the protagonist's action rather than only supply information?
- Do major choices cause subsequent turns, and does the protagonist's costly action earn the climax and resolution?
- Are setups, payoffs, stakes, and scene consequences legible without an external plan or explanation?
- Do character voices and dramatized choices support the apparent arcs, or does the narration merely assert transformation?
- Do chapter order and scene proportions sustain momentum without convenient rescues or a faux climax?

### Clarity and agency

- Is the intended actor and action clear in each sentence?
- Are pronoun antecedents, dialogue speakers, and spatial relationships unambiguous?
- Are passive constructions and abstract phrasing obscuring useful information?

### POV, tense, and continuity of presentation

- Does narration stay within the selected point of view and knowledge available in the scene?
- Are tense changes intentional and readable?
- Are invented terms, capitalization, spelling, and formatting consistent?

### Rhythm and repetition

- Do sentence and paragraph lengths support the intended pace and emphasis?
- Are repeated words, sentence openings, constructions, or transition patterns distracting?
- Are filter words, adverbs, sticky constructions, or crutch phrases weakening otherwise effective passages?

### Scene prose and dialogue

- Do dialogue tags and beats identify the speaker without drawing unnecessary attention?
- Is character emotion conveyed with enough concrete action, sensation, or thought for the scene?
- Are clichés, vague qualifiers, and generic descriptions replacing specific observation?
- Does exposition arrive when the reader can understand and use it?

### Proportion and polish

- Do paragraphs and scenes spend space in proportion to their narrative importance?
- Are grammar, punctuation, and readability problems interrupting comprehension?
- Do stylistic irregularities appear intentional and consistent with voice rather than errors to normalize mechanically?

## Fresh reader

Inputs: actual manuscript units in reading order, target reader description, these reader-experience questions, and only this reviewer's own reader-state after the first unit. Read without Story Bible, plan, prior reviews, other perspectives, or author explanation.

### Comprehension

- Where are events, motivations, stakes, setting, or relationships unclear?
- Where must you reread to identify a speaker, actor, time, place, or causal link?
- What important expectation or question remains unanswered at the end?

### Engagement

- Where does attention rise or fall, and at what exact passage?
- Where does the story feel rushed, repetitive, confusing, or slow in the moment of reading?
- Where do you feel compelled to continue or tempted to stop?

### Emotional response

- Which turning points, losses, reveals, or resolutions land, and which feel unearned?
- Where is a character's reaction difficult to believe from the information in the manuscript?
- Does the ending satisfy the expectations created by the opening and major turns?

### Target-reader fit

- Where would the stated target reader have missing context or unwanted assumptions?
- Which promises implied by the manuscript itself are fulfilled or frustrated?
- What would you describe as the story's central conflict and emotional journey after reading, using only the manuscript?

Report experience as observed at a location. Avoid inventing an author intention and then grading against it.
