# Chapter 1: The Last Check-Out

At 23:10, the Tide Archive announced that it had lost twelve years of voices.

Ning Lin did not hear the building speak, although she later used that sentence when she told the story. She heard one soft chime from the master drive. The green copy light stopped at 03:17:42. On the monitor, every file after 2014 turned the same flat gray as the demolition notice nailed beside the door.

There was salt on the window. There was rain needling the glass. There was a strip of red tape across the ferry turnstile, and there was her mother's empty chair behind the information desk. The room seemed to be arranged around the absences.

Ning pressed COPY again. The drive considered her request and produced the error in a courteous white box.

"CATALOG INDEX UNAVAILABLE. SOURCE MEDIA MAY BE DEGRADED."

Below the error, the active job still carried its original title: `MEI LIN / FINAL DEPOSIT / DO NOT DELETE`. The file had no duration and no reel reference. Ning had created the entry eleven months ago, three days after the funeral, and had not opened it.

Her phone lay beside the keyboard. For one second the dark screen reflected her face, and she saw the other screen, the one that had lit up at 21:46 on the last Thursday of Mei's life. Ning had been shelving school interviews. She had looked at her mother's name, remembered the argument about selling the house, and put the phone face down.

She turned this phone face down too.

The outer door rattled. Tao stood under the broken awning with a plastic case hugged to his chest. Rain shone on his shaved head. He held up his free hand, showing two fingers, then pointed at his watch.

Ning opened the door exactly wide enough for her shoulder.

"You are early," she said.

"You asked for twenty minutes of electricity. Han gave you thirty." Tao lifted the case. "The digitizer has its own battery. Two channels. We take your mother's reel and whatever is on the priority list, and we go."

"The priority list has four hundred and twelve entries."

"Then tonight it has one."

A ferry horn sounded across the bay. It was not a real ferry; the last boat had left at eighteen hundred. It was Track 1 of the archive's welcome loop, playing automatically for a lobby with no visitors. Ning had meant to disable it. Now the recorded note traveled through the empty ticket hall and returned from the rafters half a second late.

Tao looked toward the speaker. "That thing used to scare me."

"You were six. Everything scared you."

"You were nine. You held my hand so hard I lost circulation."

For a moment Ning's grip loosened on the door. Then the master drive chimed again.

Tao set the plastic case on the turnstile. "I can image the drive first. It may not be the media. The controller could be loose."

"And if you touch it and the index disappears completely?"

"Then we still have the reels. That is why they are reels."

"We do not have the reference for hers."

He said nothing. That was how she knew he had already read the priority list over her shoulder. The silence made her feel exposed, so she became very precise.

"Mei's deposit was made during the route-closure interviews. Cabinet C, probably. The accession ledger puts it between a harbor pilot and a school choir, but neither number matches. The digital entry was manually created. A cross-reference was omitted. The error is recoverable if I follow procedure."

"Ning."

"The tape is not missing. It is misfiled."

"Okay. Let me help you find it."

The kindness in his voice was worse than an order. He had flown in from the mainland that afternoon. He had a hotel room, a return ticket, a laptop full of sea-wall calculations. He could stand in the rain and be kind because leaving had taught him how to put a boundary around a place.

Ning looked at the portable digitizer. Accepting it would mean accepting his sequence: one reel, one copy, one exit. It would mean that after eleven months of not listening, she might walk out with the wrong four hundred voices still inside.

"You should go back to the hotel," she said.

Tao's expression changed. "That is not a plan."

"It is procedure."

He put one boot against the bottom of the door before she could close it. "The wall moved two centimeters in the last survey. Not the building. The sea wall. Han can stop a demolition crew. He cannot stop the bay."

"I know how water works."

"Then stop acting as if concrete is a moral position."

She pushed the door. He removed his boot rather than trap it, and she hated him briefly for making the safer choice look like another thing he had done to her.

The bolt slid home between them.

"Ning." His palm struck the glass once. "Open the door."

She pulled the archive ledger from the information desk and wrote a new checkout line: `23:41 — complete analog transfer — N. Lin`. Under DUE she wrote `05:30`.

There was no trolley for the reels. There were nine cabinets and perhaps six working hours. There was no staff electrician, no second deck calibrated for transfer, no tested backup of the catalog. She made a list of the missing resources because a list converted panic into inventory.

Then she unplugged the welcome loop, silencing the ferry horn halfway through its note.

The silence was immediate. It was not peaceful. It was the silence after a telephone stops ringing.

Ning took the flashlight from under the desk and walked into Cabinet A. Behind her, Tao's plastic case remained on the turnstile. He had left the digitizer and taken himself out of her plan.

She decided she would forgive him after she had saved everything.
